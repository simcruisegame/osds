# v2.0.4 trees, steps 2 and 3: the right species in stands, and no pop or hitch

These follow step 1, which is in v2.0.3: colour per crown from the aerial photograph, rounded crown light, a darker crown inside and at the trunk foot.

## Step 2: species from LANDFIRE EVT, in stands

**Data.**
- `data/landfire/evt-lf2022-conus-names.json`: 798 EVT codes and their names.
- `data/landfire/evt-tree-groups.json`: each code to one of 14 groups (or `none`):

| Group | Classes |
|---|---|
| oak | 72 |
| maple | 15 |
| southern pine | 48 |
| ponderosa (western pines) | 13 |
| Douglas fir | 14 |
| spruce-fir | 18 |
| aspen-birch | 5 |
| cottonwood-willow (wet ground) | 83 |
| live oak (evergreen oaks, Florida hammocks) | 40 |
| pinyon-juniper (and mesquite) | 13 |
| palm | 2 |
| redwood | 1 |
| hemlock-rhododendron | 8 |
| none (grass, shrub, crops, developed, water, rock) | 466 |

Spot checks: the Dragon's 7315 Southern Appalachian Oak Forest is oak, 7318 Cove Forest is maple, and 7353 Low-Elevation Pine is southern pine.

**The per-tile codes.**
- They come through the data access layer: `helper/data-access.mjs` source `evt`, one 80 × 80 px export per z14 tile (about 30 m a pixel), cached 365 days.
- They are capped at 400 live requests a day; a pack from the LANDFIRE bulk file comes later.
- A new helper command `species x y` returns the z16 tile's 20 × 20 group grid (one byte a cell, the majority group of the 30 m pixels).

**Placement in the game (`SimCruiseTrees.cpp`).**
1. Each measured crown takes the group of its cell.
2. Cells marked `none` or `developed` (crowns in towns, along roads, in yards) take the Köppen/region fallback: today's `ConiferShare`, extended to the groups (street trees: oak and maple in the East, live oak in the South, ponderosa and juniper in the dry West).
3. **Stands, not a coin flip.** The two broad forms (broadleaf, conifer) keep the group's own mix (southern pine forest: 80 % pine, 20 % oak), but the choice follows a 25 m value noise seeded by the world position. Neighbouring trees share their kind, like real stands, and the mix holds over a tile.
4. Wet ground (the cottonwood-willow group, or a crown within 30 m of mapped water) takes the riparian form near the water.

**Until the step-4 generator.** The 14 groups map onto the meshes we have: broadleaf (oak, maple, aspen, cottonwood, live oak), conifer (the pines, firs, hemlock, redwood, juniper) and palm. Each group gets its own tint and height-to-width rule: aspen narrow, live oak wide and low, juniper short and round. Step 4 replaces the meshes, not the choice.

**Fast test `World.TreeSpecies`.** Recorded tiles (CPU):
- the Dragon is mostly oak and pine, not spruce;
- Snake River Overlook (WY) is lodgepole, spruce-fir and aspen;
- Lincoln (NE) street trees are broadleaf;
- no `none` cell grows a forest tree outside town;
- neighbouring crowns share their kind 70 % of the time or more (stands).

**Cost.** 0 ms GPU; one 20 × 20 lookup per crown on the CPU.

## Step 3: no pop, no hitch

1. **Dither out at the far end** over 540-600 m (the ring now ends at 600 m with a hard cut). The far level fades out with the same screen noise as the level cross-fades.
2. **Shadows end at 80-96 m.** The near and mid rings cast shadows only to there, fading over the band, by a per-ring shadow distance and the material's shadow-pass mask; the far rings cast none.
   - At the Dragon, release-10 spent +2.1 ms in ShadowDepths in the driver's seat (efficiency-report.md, saving #2). This takes back most of it. Off: `sc.Trees.ShadowFar 0`.
3. **One tree component per tile, built off the game thread.** Each tile's crowns, colours and transforms are worked out on a worker (they need only the tile build, the canopy rows and the aerial). The game thread then swaps one HISM per tile and level in, instead of clearing and refilling six rings for all 49 tiles on every canopy arrival.
   - Today's full refresh (`Refresh()`, about 30-60 ms at 8,000 trees) is the hitch on tile arrival. After this, a tile's trees cost one component swap.
4. **The trunk pool** keeps its 32 nearest capsules, refreshed from the per-tile lists.

**Fast tests.**
- `World.TreeFade`: unit tests of the fade bands.
- `World.TreeRefresh`: the game-thread time of a tile's swap is under 2 ms at 1,500 trees.
- The GPU check (tree shadow pass under budget at the Dragon) waits for a GPU slot.

**Order.** Step 3 before step 2 if the GPU budget is the hurry (it saves ms); step 2 first if the look is. Both are about 1.5 days.
