# Race tracks

The pause menu's **Race tracks** row lists 22 oval tracks and 14 road courses (among them the Charlotte Roval, the Indianapolis road course and the Long Beach street circuit) across the US. Choose one and the car starts on its start/finish straight, facing the way the races run. The tracks are empty: no traffic and no parked cars.

OSDS is not affiliated with or endorsed by any track or racing series. Track names identify the places; there are no track or series logos, no sponsor names and no lettering in the world.

## How a track is built

Everything that says where a track is comes from OpenStreetMap, live, like every road in the game:

- **The racing surface** is the map's `highway=raceway` ways (the vector tiles' class `raceway`), drawn as road ribbons on the elevation like any road (`helper/road-widths.mjs`, `helper/road-grade.mjs`, `FOsdsRoadNetwork`). One-way ways give the racing direction.
- **Width.** OpenStreetMap rarely maps a raceway's width and the vector tiles drop it, so each track has a typical width by kind: superspeedways 18 m, intermediate ovals 16 m, short ovals 15 m, road courses 13 m; pit lanes are at most 12 m. These are estimates, not measurements.
- **Banking.** An oval's published banking (turns, frontstretch, backstretch; the track's Wikipedia infobox, checked November 2026) is applied by where each point of the track lies on the oval's loop: the turn figure where the loop curves most, the straight figures on the straights, eased between by the loop's curvature, and raised on the outside of the oval. Because it depends only on position, the two tiles on each side of a seam bank the same. Road courses that leave the oval (Daytona's, Charlotte's Roval, Indianapolis's) ease to flat within 25 m of the loop; the pit lane stays flat. Where a track's turns differ (Darlington 25/23°, Texas 20/24°, Pocono 14/8/6°) one figure is used for all of them; the table says which.
- **The loop and the start** are made once by `scripts/tracks/curate.py` from the OpenStreetMap ways: the loop is the outer boundary of the oval's raceway lines (its infield road course, pit lane, kart and dirt tracks left out by name); the start is the point of the loop nearest the middle of the pit lane, which is the start/finish straight at nearly every US circuit. Ovals run counter-clockwise; road courses run the way their mapped one-way ways run (Laguna Seca and COTA counter-clockwise, the others clockwise). The mapped loop length is checked against the published length (`OSDS.World.Tracks.Data`).
- **Lines, walls and fences** (`OsdsTrackDetail`): white edge lines; on an oval the double yellow line at the bottom of the banking; a white start/finish line at the start point; on every oval stretch, the outside wall with a SAFER barrier face, concrete behind it and a 6.7 m catch fence of posts and cables on top; a pit wall between the pit road and the frontstretch. Road courses get red and white kerbs on the inside of every corner tighter than 140 m radius. Walls and fence posts collide.
- **Not modelled yet:** per-turn banking where it differs, variable banking across the width (Bristol's progressive banking, Homestead's), the apron as a separate surface, the track surface's own texture (it is the road asphalt), the rubbered-in racing line, grandstands beyond the buildings OpenStreetMap maps, run-off gravel and tyre walls on road courses.

## Using it

- Pad: Menu, then **Race tracks** (the flag), then **Oval tracks** or **Road courses**, then the track. B goes back.
- Command line: `-OsdsTrack=<id>` starts at a track (`-OsdsTrack=daytona`); the ids are in `data/tracks/tracks.json`.
- Tests: `OSDS.World.Tracks.Data`, `OSDS.World.Tracks.Banking` (offline) and `OSDS.Network.Tracks.StartResolves` (needs the map helper and the network: every track's start resolves to its racing surface, within 60 m of the line, facing the racing direction).

## Updating the list

`uv run --no-project --with shapely python scripts/tracks/curate.py <cache dir>` fetches each track's raceway ways from Overpass (once, cached) and writes `data/tracks/tracks.json`. Names, lengths and banking are the curated part at the top of the script; change them there, with the source.
