# v2.0.4: bridge clearance across tile seams (Northcrest Road over I-285)

## Why release-12 could not fix it

Plan A decides each crossing inside one z16 tile, from that tile's own ribbons.
- A cut or lift ramp has to stay inside the ribbons: `Fits()` keeps every ramp off a ribbon end and off a seam pin.
- Northcrest Road over I-285 needs 7.1 m. That takes about 340 m of ramps on either road, and both roads leave the tile sooner.
- If one tile changed a road's height near the seam, the neighbour tile (built on its own, in any order) would not know, and the road would step at the seam.

Round 2 (cut and lift shared, the deck kept whole) and round 3 (deck-over-deck by OSM layer) leave 698 faults at Doraville and 143 in Detroit. Almost all of them are on spans that are left as they were for this reason.

## Design: the crossing is decided once, from data that both tiles see, and applied by distance along the road

1. **The helper finds the crossings per z14 tile**, the 4 x 4 z16 game tiles that share one vector tile.
   - A crossing is a deck way over another way at a different OSM layer (or a deck over an undecked road).
   - For a crossing near a z14 edge, the neighbouring z14 tiles are read, as `road-grade.mjs` already does for roads that leave the tile.
   - Each crossing gets a stable id: a hash of both ways' geometry rounded to 0.5 m and their classes and layers. The tile data carries no OSM way ids, so geometry stands in for them, as the bridge-end memo already does.
2. **The record:** id, crossing point (lon/lat), both roads' polylines out to 500 m each way, their classes and layers, and the deck and road heights at the crossing (the DEM and the bridge-end memo, as the game's deck line uses them).
   - The decision follows plan A's rules: cut the lower road (a motorway or trunk, or a lower layer), else lift the deck, else share in tenths. The ramp lengths come from the grade limits (4 % for a cut, 6 % for a lift).
   - Ramps are no longer limited by the tile. They are limited only by the road's own junctions within 500 m (a ramp never passes a junction).
3. **The game applies it by distance along the road.** Each z16 tile's helper document lists the records whose ramps reach it.
   - A tile changes the height of every ribbon station that lies on a record's road (within 1 m of its polyline, same class) by the record's offset at that station's distance from the crossing point: a smoothstep plateau and ramps, as today.
   - Both tiles at a seam evaluate the same function of world position, so the seam pin gets the same offset on both sides and nothing steps.
   - The deck itself still comes from the game's deck line; the record only adds its offset.
4. **Junctions:** a ramp that would reach a junction stops at a plateau before it (2 m), and the junction road joins at the shifted height through the existing junction join.

## Fast test

`World.BridgeDecks` with the record:
- no "left as it was" at Doraville or Detroit Woodward;
- Northcrest Road clears I-285 by 4.9 m;
- across every seam of a record's road, the heights on both sides agree within 1 cm.

Plus a two-tile test that builds tile A then B, and B then A, and checks the seam heights are the same (no build-order dependence).

## Example

Northcrest Road over I-285, Doraville GA (33.8938, -84.2610), and the review lane's eight bridge-type places in `Saved\CivilPlaces`.

## Cost and risk

About 2 days.
- The helper needs the deck heights the game's deck line uses: the bridge ends from the memo, straight between them, the same smoothing. That logic moves to shared rules, or the helper sends the inputs and the game computes, keyed by the record id.
- The bridge P1s the review lane found on release-12 must stay fixed: no deck-end step (ease over the ramp) and no road dip to hide a road (the hide rule goes).
