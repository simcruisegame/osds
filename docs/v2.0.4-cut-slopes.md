# v2.0.4: cut and fill slopes that look built by nature and by road crews, not by a cap

**Now (v2.0.3, 60cb4b7).** The ground never stands over a carriageway. The cap holds it under the road surface out to one ground-cell diagonal (3.1 m) past the edge. Where the natural ground is a hillside, the next vertex out keeps its natural height. So the cut is a sheer face 3 m from the lane: flat, vertical and textured like the hillside's aerial photograph.
- Review frame: `C:\sc\final\verify-bridges\60cb4b7-bixby-south-190m.jpg`, CA-1 south of Bixby Creek.
- Real road cuts have a ditch at the toe and a slope whose angle follows the material, rounded into the ground at the top: rock, benches and talus on hard rock; grass on soil.

## General rule: the cross-section beyond the edge

Built in the ground conditioning (`SimCruiseTileBuilder`), out from each carriageway edge, wherever the natural ground rises above the road (a cut):

1. **Shoulder and ditch at the toe.** A 0.5-1.5 m shoulder by road class, then a ditch 0.3-0.5 m deep with a 0.6 m bottom (the existing rural section's foreslope and ditch, now for every road in a cut, urban kerbs excepted).
2. **The cut slope, by material:**

| Material | Slope (horizontal : vertical) | Benches |
|---|---|---|
| Hard rock | 0.25:1 to 0.5:1 | On cuts over 10 m, a 2-3 m bench every 8-10 m of height (FHWA practice) |
| Weathered or soft rock | 1:1 | None |
| Soil | 1.5:1 to 2:1 | None |

3. **The top edge is rounded** into the natural ground over 2-4 m (a parabola, as cuts are trimmed), so there is no crease.
4. **Fill,** where the road is above the ground, keeps today's 1:2 (2:1 in this notation), with a rounded toe.
5. **The ground-never-over-road cap stays the last write**, and its edge reach is widened to the ditch, so no triangle touches a lane. Slope shapes only ever lower the ground relative to the natural surface in a cut; they never raise it.

**Material.** All sources are public domain, read through the data access layer (`helper/data-access.mjs`): per-tile packs later, a capped live fetch now.
- **USGS SGMC** (State Geologic Map Compilation) gives the lithology polygons: granite, schist, sandstone, shale, alluvium and so on, mapped to hard rock, soft rock or unconsolidated.
- **gSSURGO / SSURGO** depth to bedrock: under 1 m means rock close below, so rock slopes start at that depth in the cut (soil over rock: soil slope on top, rock slope below).
- **Fallback:** the slope of the natural ground. Over 35° means rock; 15-35° means weathered rock; under 15° means soil.

**Look.**
- **Rock faces:** a rock material with strata (oriented by the SGMC dip where known, else horizontal), large-scale noise and drill-hole lines on hard rock cuts. Its colour is taken from the aerial photograph of the natural slope beside the cut (the same photo sampling as the tree crowns), so Big Sur's cuts are buff and grey and the Appalachian ones brown and grey.
- **Talus** at the toe of rock cuts: a ground tint and small scattered rock instances. It is cheap and has an off switch.
- **Soil slopes** take the grass and shrub ground material (and the step-5 bushes from the tree plan).
- **Benches** carry a strip of grass.
