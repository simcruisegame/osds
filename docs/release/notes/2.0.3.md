# OSDS 2.0.3 (2026-11-15): full release notes

The short version is in [CHANGELOG.md](../../../CHANGELOG.md). This is the text first published on the GitHub release.


- **Trees, each its own colour** — every crown takes its colour from the aerial photograph at the crown (against the tile's crowns, so the photo's own exposure cancels), with a small per-tree hue and brightness change; crowns are lit as one rounded mass, darker deep inside; bark is darker at the foot. `-OsdsOldTreeLook` turns it off.
- **Walls built of something** — brick coursing, lap siding, concrete block, ashlar stone, concrete panels and ribbed metal, drawn in the building shader: the building's mapped material first (OpenStreetMap, Wikidata), else the region's and the building type's usual material (siding on northern houses, brick in the South, stucco in the South-west and Florida, block and metal for warehouses). Each material has its own colours.
- **Windows by the wall's role** — street fronts keep every bay; side and back walls fewer, houses' windows in pairs, garages and metal sheds almost none.
- **Doors** — one on each building's main street side, set between the windows and recessed, with a casing; houses have a stoop and steps up to their floor (higher in the North, lower in the dry South-west and Florida); shops and offices a glass door and a step; garages a panel door.
- **Kinder to the public servers** — a failing map server is left alone for 15 s doubling to 6 h; at most two requests at a time per server; one generic User-Agent naming OSDS; the place facts (Overpass, Wikidata) are only fetched with `-OsdsLive`; the planes poll (adsb.lol) is off with the planes (`sc.Feature.Planes`); tile retries grow to 15 min; a missing snow tile is not asked again for a day.
- First-minute fixes from the Detroit review: far-away towers keep their windows instead of turning flat grey; windows no longer glow white by day (a dimmer room behind the glass, dark frames on dark walls); street-light poles stand in the ground instead of floating over a lower verge; lane lines stay on their own road (never on the sidewalk or across the lanes of the road beside them). New test `OSDS.World.MarkingsAndPoles`.
- Building tops: the windows fit the wall. A top row of windows was cut by the roof line (it looked like battlements on long low brick buildings in Detroit), shop glass ran up to the roof, and towers had windows to their very top. Now window rows sit on whole storeys only, with a plain band of wall above the last row (0.4 m or more), a plain fascia above shop glass, a darker coping along flat roofs' parapets, and a glass tower's top band in its frame colour. New test `OSDS.World.BuildingTops` (no cut rows on the Miami Beach tiles or at Detroit's Woodward Avenue).
- Ground and trees, closer to the real place: the grass, soil, sand and paving take their colours from the place's own aerial photograph (colour matching, no AI; `-OsdsNoTint` turns it off), the ground's fine pattern costs less to draw and runs on across tile borders, steep ground shows rock from 30 degrees, and each tree stands where the canopy map shows its crown, turned and sized to it (`-OsdsOldCrowns` goes back to the old placement). Trees fade between their detail levels at the right distance instead of popping.
- Fix: the Miata is ready in about a second instead of 30-37 s. Fitting its steering wheel looked up each triangle's material role (a string match) for hundreds of thousands of triangles; the roles are now looked up once per material. The rig notes each step's time (`rig step times`) in the build log.
- Fix: no 29 s freeze when a drive starts in a Miata. The game thread waited for the car model's worker-thread part (its 38 MB glTF and rig) and Windows called the game Not Responding; the loading screen now keeps drawing until that part is done, and only the meshes are built on the game thread. Parked-car models wait for theirs the same way.
- Fix: the Corvette's digital speed reads cleanly. Its seven-segment digits had hard edges, which aliased into garble at the few pixels a cluster digit covers from the seat (Jasper review); each segment's edge is now one screen pixel wide. The cooked game's copy of the material (`M_OsdsCarScreen`) is saved again by the world-materials commandlet.
- Fix: the clutch no longer slips in every gear. The self-shifting manuals slipped their clutch to hold a launch speed whenever the road turned the engine slower than it, in any gear: the Mustang ran 4672 rpm in 3rd at 36 mph against the road's 1900, and the rev counter jumped with the pedal. Now only pulling away (1st and reverse) slips; a torque-converter automatic still slips in any gear, as a converter does.
- Fix: the automatic changes gear by its output shaft's speed (the driven wheels), as a gearbox's controller does, not by the engine's, and changes up at the limiter (the Corvette sat at its fuel cut in 5th at 153 mph; it now reaches 198 mph in 7th against Chevrolet's 194). Through a shift and with the clutch slipping the engine runs apart from the road: it fell to idle and called one downshift after another (the Dragon review: 19 mph in first at 851 rpm). And no change back the other way within 2 s of the last one unless the engine is at its limiter (the Dragon's D5-D4-D2-D4 and 1-2-1-2 hunting as the pedal moved). First gear at full pedal still runs to 6900 rpm, as the car file's upshift point says (the 1.8's redline is 7000; 1st at 6900 rpm is 34 mph). New bench `OSDS.Vehicle.ShiftDrive.*` (a moving pedal, braking down, pulling away: no change back within 2 s, the engine within 8 % of the road's speed in gear).
- Sound: the Corvette's engine is behind your back. Its intake sits about 0.9 m from the driver's ear (mid engine, from the car's geometry) against about 2 m for an engine ahead of the dash, so in the cabin its induction roar is as loud as outside instead of halved. Per-car engine timbre waits for licensed recordings: Wikimedia Commons has none of a stock C8 (only one with an aftermarket exhaust), a 1965 289 or an NB 1.8 (only a 1.6), so the current voices stay and no timbre is invented.
- Driving: each wheel knows what it stands on: asphalt, concrete (the kerb's gutter, face and top, and the sidewalk), gravel (OSM unpaved roads) or grass (the ground beside the road), and whether it is on a kerb (lifted more than 3 cm by a step, or on a kerb's slope). The road noise uses it: concrete rolls 3 dB louder and brighter (tined concrete measures 3-5 dB over asphalt in FHWA's on-board sound intensity studies), and a kerb thumps. The wind noise now follows the air past the car (the live wind), so a head wind is louder than a tail wind. The controller rumble can read the same fields (`Wheels[i].SurfaceKind`, `Wheels[i].bKerb`).
- Driving: thin air takes power. A naturally aspirated engine's torque now follows the air's density (the live temperature and the ISA pressure at the car's height, as the drag already did), against the sea-level air the cars are calibrated in: about 15 % less at Denver's 1600 m, the familiar 3 % per 1000 ft. Turbocharged engines (`"aspiration": "turbo"` in the car file: the CR-V and the Raptor) hold their boost. Test `OSDS.Vehicle.Model.AltitudePower`.
- Tests: new benches against published figures. Top speed (`OSDS.Vehicle.TopSpeed.*`: the Corvette Stingray Z51 194 mph, Chevrolet), passing (`OSDS.Vehicle.Passing.*`: the Z51 30-50 mph 1.8 s and 50-70 mph 2.5 s, Car and Driver), the 1965 Mustang's 24.7 mph per 1000 rpm in top (Car Life), and body roll per g in the step steer. Where no figure is published (the NB's and the Mustang's top speed and passing times, roll per g) the result is logged, not judged.
- Test robot: steers with the stick that gives the wheel angle it wants. It sent its wanted share of the steering range straight to the stick, but a thumb's stick goes through the dead zones and the fine centre (the stick's middle for lane corrections), so a 0.28 stick asked for about a third of the angle meant: in the 25 mph left before Bixby Creek Bridge it ran wide onto the cut bank at the right speed. The stick is now found through the same shaping (`OsdsShaping::PadStickForCommand`; test `OSDS.Gameplay.Steering.RangeMatchesGodot`).
- Fix: no "ND" emblem on the NB Miata's steering wheel. The model draws it in its instrument texture, so it was kept still with the gauges, and it is the badge of a later generation. Instrument-material triangles on the hub itself (within 30 % of the rim's radius, no deeper than 3 cm behind the rim) are removed; the cluster behind the wheel is untouched. The build log says how many (`instrument-material triangles on the hub removed`).
- Test robot: brakes for a bend by the deceleration the bend asks for (lift, then the pedal for that deceleration, full pedal taken as the car's grip), not only once it is already too fast. Braking on the speed error alone trailed its own plan by the error that made the pedal: at Bixby Creek on CA-1 it entered a 27 mph left at 33 mph with 0.36 brake and ran wide onto the cut bank.
- Fix: traction control also sees a driven wheel off the ground. It judged slip from tyre force only, so a wheel with no load was ignored: the Corvette beached on a wall end at Doraville sat at 6600 rpm in first at 0 mph with its rear wheels spinning free at full pedal. Like the real system's wheel-speed sensors, a wheel turning faster than the car moves now counts as slip, with or without load, so the engine is cut back.
- Change: moving traffic is out of the game for now, so the work goes into the details of the world and the driving. No traffic cars spawn and the traffic costs nothing each frame. Parked cars stay. One switch brings it back for development: `sc.Feature.Traffic 1` (default 0, read when the world starts) or `-OsdsTraffic` on the command line. The traffic code and its tests stay in the repo.
- **About 2 ms of GPU time back on Ultra** — Lumen GI at High on Ultra (screen probes 2.6 -> 1.3 ms), moving sun-shadow pages at 0 bias, foliage reflections at High's limits, a 768 x 320 mirror capture (with mips and anti-aliasing), and a 15 ms dynamic-resolution budget. Ultra at a fixed 75 %, 1080p, traffic off, GPU frame median / 95 %: Tail of the Dragon 20.2 / 25.5 -> 18.5 / 23.7 ms, Ocean Drive 21.6 / 28.1 -> 19.6 / 25.9 ms.
- **Installer: a clean PC can play.** A clean Windows 10 or 11 has neither the Microsoft Visual C++ 2015-2022 x64 runtime (the game and 20 engine DLLs need `MSVCP140`, `MSVCP140_1`, `VCRUNTIME140` and `VCRUNTIME140_1`) nor Microsoft GameInput (racing wheels; the launcher asks for it), and the installer brought neither: on such a PC the game could not start. The package now carries Microsoft's signed installers for both (`BuildCookRun -prereqs`, `[GameInput] IncludeRedistFiles=True`, copied in by `scripts\package.ps1`; terms in `licenses/microsoft-redistributables.md`), and the installer runs each one the PC lacks (an older runtime is replaced), with one Windows permission prompt each; if the player says no, it names what is missing and how to install it, and the launcher asks again. A page before the install says what works offline (only Ocean Drive, Miami Beach), which Microsoft parts it installs, and the PC that the game needs, and that we tested on one PC only. The uninstaller now deletes the map cache, downloaded photos, other caches and logs in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\OSDS`, and asks before it deletes settings, tuning and screenshots (kept by default). New: [docs/release/clean-pc-test.md](docs/release/clean-pc-test.md), the clean-PC test steps. `docs/install.md`: honest requirements (the tested PC, the rest an estimate), memory 16 GB (was 8 GB, never tested).

- **Cockpit and mirrors** (release review) — the car's own parts cast no screen-space contact shadows (black stair-stepped streaks across the Miata binnacle and column in sun; the virtual shadow map still draws the car's shadows); the driver's view opens up to 1.5 EV in daylight (1 EV left the cabin near black in building shade); the mirror glass samples a mip-mapped capture (no more blocky, aliased picture) at 70 % (it read too bright against a cabin-exposed view) and shows its plain tint until the first capture has rendered (no 1.8 s of black glass after a stall).
- **No traffic and no aircraft in 2.0.3, and no settings for them** (`Gameplay/OsdsFeatures`) — the pause menu's Traffic row and the road atlas's Traffic button are gone, and the data credits leave out adsb.lol while no aircraft data is fetched (Open-Meteo stays). The code is kept behind `sc.Feature.Traffic` and `sc.Feature.Aircraft` (0 by default); with a switch at 1 the items come back.
- **The round map no longer covers the dash** — from the driver's seat the map is smaller (75 %) and sits in the top-left corner over the windscreen pillar and the sky, inside the safe margin, so it stays off the gauges, the clock, the Corvette's centre screen and the rear-view mirror (the Corvette's hangs at the top right); the turn sign keeps clear of it. A new setting, Settings > *Map*: *With route* (the default) shows the map only while the GPS has a route, *Always* shows it all the time; it is saved.
- **Fix: sharp speed digits** — the HUD's text was drawn from a 24 pt glyph and stretched (about four times for the 132 px speed), so the digits looked soft. Every HUD text is now drawn at its real size, on whole pixels, with a hard 2 px shadow, and the speed and gear no longer fade in Glance (only their card does). `scripts\hud-shots.ps1` takes the HUD from the driver's seat in the Miata, Mustang and Corvette, and 2x crops of the speed at 0, 60 and 120.

- Test: the pre-flight judge watches the traffic. Ten times a second it checks each traffic car against our car and fails the run for a car that cuts into our lane within one car length while we drive, a car that drives through our car, a car that turns round (150 deg or more in 3 s), or a motorway car that crosses our road at our level (Woodward Avenue over I-75 in Detroit showed one). A new robot option, `-OsdsPreflightStopAtJunction=N` (`launch-review.ps1 -StopAtJunction N`), stops at the first stop line, holds N s, then pulls out, for a gap-acceptance check; `scripts\traffic-retest.ps1` runs the traffic cases. Rules in docs/team/release-test.md ("The traffic judge"), tests `OSDS.Gameplay.Preflight.Traffic`. The bridge check now fails any lifted or de-decked span (only a faces-removed change is allowed, also at Bixby Bridge), and the release report fails a start-pick fallback line.
- **Known issues (v2.0.3)**:
  - At some motorway interchanges a bridge meets the road below at the same level instead of passing over it; real bridge clearance comes in 2.0.4.
  - Junction lips: where a driveway or a ramp joins a road, part of it stands above the road it joins: about 12 cm where two driveways meet Court Street in Welch, WV (tile 16/17916/25409, near 37.43300, -81.58373), about 6 cm at motorway ramp merges in Doraville, GA (tile 16/17429/26202, near 33.89359, -84.25539). The cause is still under study (not the join's choice of road: taking the most major road instead of the highest surface did not change them); the real fix is the first v2.0.4 item. `OSDS.Debug.ReleaseFaultProbe` shows each join's columns against the road under them.
  - A 78.8 cm step where a road meets the motorway decks of the Doraville interchange (tile 16/17429/26202, the three motorway bridges between tile coordinates 0.31-0.51 x 0.81-1.00, about 33.8935, -84.2575): a bridge approach crosses the other road under 1 m apart in height, so the step check counts the deck; the exact spot comes from the probe in the next build.
  - Miata cockpit: in direct sun, dark jagged shadow marks can show on the dash behind the steering wheel (the model's thin dash parts shadowing themselves). Fix planned for 2.0.4.
  - Ground over a lane edge: at about 300 short spots the ground stands 0.1-1.4 m over the edge of a carriageway, mostly at tile seams and junctions (`World.TerrainNeverOverRoad` counts 304 points on the 20 recorded start places, from 1,638 before the 2.0.3 fixes; it reports them and does not fail for 2.0.3).
  - Launch slip: on a full-throttle start the 1965 Mustang and the Corvette can stay at the fuel cut in 1st with the rear tyres slipping, because the automatic does not change up while the driven wheels spin. A fix (the shift guard on the output shaft) is ready for 2.0.4.
  - The Corvette's automatic can change from 1st directly to 3rd under hard acceleration from a stop.

- Fix: no bridge wall across a road. A bridge deck that met another road at its own level (a crossing at grade, a merge gore) stood its 2 m concrete parapet block, and its 1.2 m deck slab, across that carriageway: the drive robot hit Northcrest Road's parapets across I-285 in Doraville, GA at 56 mph and stopped dead. Now no parapet and no deck slab is built where it lies inside another carriageway at the deck's level (the deck from 0.7 m under to 2.5 m over the road); the rest of each span keeps them, and no road or deck changes height (at Bixby Bridge only the parapet across the mouth of Coast Road goes). Piers never stand in a road. Each span with faces left out is logged (`Bridge span <tile>/r<ribbon> at <lat>, <lon>: faces removed over N m ...`); `OSDS_NO_BRIDGE_RULE=1` turns the rule off for before/after numbers. New tests `OSDS.World.RoadOverlaps.Bundled` and `.Recorded` (slow; writes `Saved/BridgeFix/changed-spans.md`). Lifting decks to a real clearance waits for v2.0.4 (embankments).
- Fix: the start pick works in the game, not only in the tests. An installed tile hands its layers' triangles to the collision and empties them, so the pick's road-face checks (a road face under each corner, and 10 and 20 m ahead) saw no road anywhere and every start failed; the game then placed the car by the old nearest-road rule (Welch, WV: nose at the grass bank beside the brick building, 3.7 m past the edge at frame 0). Each tile build now keeps its road faces for the pick (`FOsdsTileBuild::RoadTops`, made on the worker), freed once the car is placed. When the pick finds no start, the log says so in one fixed line, `OSDS START FALLBACK: start pick found no candidate`, with the count of stations each rule left out, and the drive robot fails the run with `start-by-nearest-road-rule`. `OSDS.World.Start.OnRoadNotDeadEnd` empties the layers the way an install does and needs the same start.

- Faster start: a car model's glTF and rig (most of a heavy model's build) now run on a worker thread from the moment a drive or a trip begins, for the player's car and every traffic model; only the meshes are built on the game thread while the start tiles load. The Miata's whole build took 27.7 s on the game thread at Welch, West Virginia; the log now splits each build (`built in ... s: ... s glTF and rig, ... s meshes`). Pre-flight: the car stands on its parking brake until the drive starts (the Mustang, an automatic, crept 3.7 m onto the grass bank at Welch while the tiles and shaders finished).

- Drive robot: smooth hands. It steered from the frame-to-frame change of heading, which jumps with uneven frames and single-step contacts, and sawed the wheel in the Dragon's hairpins at 14-15 mph; it now feels a low-passed yaw rate (0.1 s) and moves the stick at most four full widths a second.

- Fix: the drive robot no longer takes a ramp merge for a dead end. Its path ended where a ramp joins the motorway's side (not at a road's end), so it braked from 28 m/s to a stand and steered to full lock at Doraville, Georgia; it now goes on along the road it meets, the way it heads (within about 50 deg). A car ahead stops it only within 1.8 m of its own line, not one beside a merge or a bend. Traffic: a car kept waiting takes a shorter gap, so a steady stream never holds it for good: a left-turner waiting in the junction for oncoming cars accepts 4.5 s, after 8 s 2.5 s, after 15 s 1.5 s, and a car at a stop sign 5 s, after 10 s 3 s, after 20 s 2 s (a Corvette waiting to turn left held the robot at a Stonington junction for a minute).

- Fix: the Miata's horn pad no longer swings at full lock. The wheel's pivot and column now come from the rim's outer edge (the farthest point from the column in each of 64 sectors), a clean circle whatever the rim is made of; the NB's leather ring and black inner ring were not concentric, and fitting the leather alone left the pivot 1.6 cm off the true centre. Rig check: Miata 0.05 cm and 1.06 deg, Corvette 0.00 cm and 0.00 deg, Mustang 0.03 cm and 0.24 deg (limits 0.5 cm and 2 deg). Traffic: a car at a dead end with no other way on turns round on the same road (Fish Pier Road, Stonington, Maine), and a car queued for 18 s (was 25) turns off out of sight, before the pre-flight's 20 s stuck limit.

- Fix: no freeze when the first traffic car appears. Every model a traffic or parked car can be (eight: the 1999 Miata, '65 Mustang, Corvette, Model 3, Grand Cherokee, F-150 Raptor, Odyssey, CR-V) is now built while the start tiles load, one a frame, and the car is placed once they are all built; the 1999 Miata's 1.2-million-triangle model, cut and rigged on the game thread when its car first appeared, froze a packaged drive for 3.3 s at the Dragon's start. Each build is logged (`Traffic model ... built before the drive in ... s`).
- **Fix: the car starts on a road that goes on, and sooner** (`World/OsdsStartPick`, release test 20261114-0725-53b290c) — the start is picked by general rules: a public road, mid-block, 25 m or more inside its tile (a seam's road ends are where a neighbour tile's road stub lies beside the drawn road: Welch, WV started 3.7 m off the road on a bank), a straight piece, in the traffic lane, the car's footprint on the carriageway with no ground above the road, and 800 m or more of road ahead the way the drive robot follows it (Stonington, ME started facing a wharf and stopped after 114 m); a ground trace confirms each start before the car goes there. Background cars (parked, traffic) no longer build a heavy model on the game thread (the Miatas' 38 MB took 30-37 s: 98-100 s starts in Detroit and Doraville, a 33 s freeze while driving in Big Sur); a lighter car takes their share until the player's choice has built it. The player's car model builds while the start tiles load (five at once until the car is placed) instead of after them.
- **Fix: no spikes in the land to the horizon** (`AOsdsRegionalTerrain::RejectHeightOutliers`) — a landscape vertex far from the median of the ground within 600 m (more than 100 m or four times its spread), with little ground near its own height, takes that median: a corrupt elevation pixel off Stonington, ME stood as a 1438 m white spike beside a -314 m pit in the sea. Mountains, plateaus and cliffs keep their relief.
- Fix: no freeze when the first traffic car appears. Every model a traffic or parked car can be ('65 Mustang, Corvette, Model 3, Grand Cherokee, F-150 Raptor, Odyssey, CR-V; the 1999 Miata only once something else has built it, see above) is now built while the start tiles load, one a frame, and the car is placed once they are all built; the 1999 Miata's 1.2-million-triangle model, cut and rigged on the game thread when its car first appeared, froze a packaged drive for 3.3 s at the Dragon's start. Each build is logged (`Traffic model ... built before the drive in ... s`).

- Fix: the button hints on every screen follow the device used last. The pause menu kept showing the keyboard's keys (Q E, Enter, Esc) while you used the pad, because each screen guessed on its own. Now one flag, set from all of Slate's input (also keys sent through `FSlateApplication` by scripts and tests), switches every screen's hints: any pad button or a stick pushed past half shows the pad's buttons, any key, mouse button or mouse move the keyboard's. The road atlas, Where to?, Tune car and Seat & view help lines switch too. New test `OSDS.UI.PadHints`.

- **Fix: no more road steps where two roads meet at an angle** (`OsdsRoadNetwork` junction joins) — a road joining another took the other road's height column by column, but the columns beside a snapped one moved by one station average, so a lane could rise up to 65 cm in half a metre (5th Street into MacArthur Causeway, Miami Beach). Each column now fades from its own points on the other road. A road-step check measures every road of a tile every 10 cm along its centre line and wheel lines (`OSDS.World.RoadSteps.BundledTiles`, `-OsdsRoadCheck` logs it per tile): 381 steps over 2 cm on the 24 Miami tiles before, 260 now (worst 65 -> 33 cm; the rest are service-road joins and ramps that overlap a causeway with no bridge in the data). Ocean Drive pre-flight (keen Miata, Ultra): 2 road steps and 11 jolts before, 0 and 5 now.
- **Tiles load ahead at highway speed** (`OsdsTileManager`) — the tiles on the line to where the car will be (speed x (2 x the measured load time + 4 s)) load first and always keep one of the two load slots; the side tiles follow. At 130 km/h with 8 s loads every tile is ready at least 4.4 s before the car reaches it on any heading (`OSDS.World.TileManager.HighwayLookAhead`); before, with 10 s loads the car arrived a second early. A tile in range that has no ground yet (still loading, or its load failed) gets a flat collision slab at its neighbours' edge height, so the car never falls through (`-OsdsFailTiles=x_y` forces a failure for tests).
- **Real climate, not a latitude band** — the Köppen-Geiger 1991-2020 classes (Beck et al. 2023, CC BY 4.0) are bundled as a 0.05 degree global grid (`data/climate`, 0.57 MB). Each tile takes its class (Miami Beach Am, Tail of the Dragon Cfa, Big Sur Csb) for the place graph, the season, the grass dryness (November lawns in Tennessee turn tan, Miami stays green) and the trees it expects. Every 32 m of road also gets a context record (surface, curbs, sidewalks, guard rails, land use either side from OpenStreetMap, else rules) that palms, hydrants and the grass read.
- Guard rails where OpenStreetMap maps them (`barrier=guard_rail`, from the 32 m road record), as well as where the ground drops away.
- **Rural roadsides with a ditch and a cut, no neon fields, roofs as the photograph shows them** — beyond a rural shoulder the ground now falls along a 1:4 foreslope into a 0.45 m ditch, then a 1:1.5 cut climbs into the hill, or a 1:2 fill drops to the valley side (`OSDS.World.RuralCrossSection`). Lawns and meadows keep a plant's albedo and saturation, so a field never glows lime green. Flat and pitched roofs take their colour from the aerial photograph where it shows enough of them (`OSDS.World.Buildings.RoofColourFromAerial`).
- Fix: no more black tears on the Miata's spokes when the wheel is turned. They were the turning wheel's own shadow (gone with its shadow off, still there with its shadow pages redrawn every frame), so the cockpit wheel casts no shadow; under the cowl the dash's shadow covers that area anyway (`-OsdsWheelShadow` turns it back on). The wheel's slab also reaches as deep as its spokes (8.5 cm). Pre-flight: a traffic car waiting behind the player, or standing since before the drive began, is no longer counted as stuck (both cars the release test flagged at the Dragon were queued behind the player's car at the start).

- **Fix: a black dash stays black under trees** — from the driver's seat the camera may open at most 1.5 EV in daylight (`DriverDaylightOpenStops`; outside views and captures keep 3 EV). Metering the shaded cabin and forest opened 3 EV, so the sunlit near-black Miata dash and wheel came out light grey-tan (owner's screenshot). The Tail of the Dragon realism score is unchanged (45.6).
- Fix: the lock-to-lock turns now reach the game. They sat in the wrong part of the car files and were never read, so the cockpit wheel still took ratio x lock (549 deg in the Miata); a rig check now fails a car file without them. The Miata's wheel takes the trim and leather welded to its column that lies in the wheel's own slab (5,736 triangles that sat still on the spokes as the wheel turned; 74 near the column stay). Its rim fit keeps a fixed tube about the first pass's radius. The tachometer needle follows the engine in 0.04 s (a gear change no longer leaves it hundreds of r/min behind). The drive robot waits for a crossing car on the parking brake: the brake trigger held at a standstill selects reverse, and it had reversed away from a car crossing at Ocean Drive. Open: small tears where the Miata's upper spokes meet the horn pad when the wheel is turned.

- **Fix: every menu works with an Xbox controller, the page tabs at the top included** — the pause menu's tabs (Drive, Your car, Seat & view, Settings) could not be reached with the pad: they were not controls, and Up from the first row jumped to the last row. A mouse cursor resting on the menu also took the selection back whenever the page changed under it. Now Up from the first row goes onto the tabs (left and right move along them, A or Down opens one, with a white ring on the tab), LB and RB change the page from anywhere, and every page opens on its first row. The pad's place is Slate's focus, so it cannot be lost: a still mouse no longer moves it, and the game puts it back if it goes. The road atlas's top buttons (zoom, Recenter, Drive here, Back, Find a place, Surprise me, car, gearbox and all 14 settings) are reached with the D-pad, amber with a white ring while chosen. Where to? has a pad keyboard (A on the search box; X deletes, Y types a space) and the D-pad walks the box, its buttons, the places and Back. B now goes back one screen (to the menu row that opened it) and Menu closes everything. One push of the left stick is one row (it moved two). The map data credits (Where to?) add "Weather by Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0) · aircraft by adsb.lol (ODbL)". ## Known issues

- No other traffic and no aircraft in this version: both are switched off while the world and the driving get the attention (the code stays in the game behind `sc.Feature.Traffic` and `sc.Feature.Aircraft`).
- Facades: most walls use one window pattern; doors and shopfronts appear only on mapped shop streets. More facade variety is planned for 2.0.4.
- One street-light model, and trees from three species meshes, so both repeat. More species and fixtures are planned for 2.0.4.
- Xbox impulse triggers do not rumble: the game uses the standard XInput path, which has no trigger motors. The two main motors carry the surface, kerb, slip and ABS feel.
