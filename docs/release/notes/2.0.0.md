# SimCruise 2.0.0 (2026-11-12): full release notes

The short version is in [CHANGELOG.md](../../../CHANGELOG.md).

The first full release of the Unreal Engine 5.8 client, for Windows: a Windows installer (per-user or all users, Start menu entry, uninstaller) and a portable zip, with SHA-256 checksums. Drive anywhere in the US in a 2001 Miata, a 1965 Mustang, a Corvette C8 and six other cars. The release gate (`docs/release/driving-checklist.md`, results in `docs/release/v2.0.0-report.md`) checked the cars against published road tests, the pad and wheel input, cameras, sound, traffic, crashes, frame rate and input delay on a laptop GPU, starting anywhere, settings, night driving, the cockpit field of view and a 20-minute drive in the Shipping build.

Known issues in 2.0.0:

- The Corvette C8 reaches 60 mph in 3.22 s (with a 1 ft rollout); Car and Driver measured 2.8 s. Its stop and skidpad match.
- The 1965 Mustang spins in a full-lock launch at 70 % trigger; the open differential should let the inside wheel spin instead.
- The Tesla Model 3 can spin when it trail-brakes hard at 0.7 g: the game has no stability-control model yet.
- An offline start was tested with the live feeds blocked (`-SimCruiseLiveOffline`), not with the whole network off.
- On an unlit rural road at night the low beams light the road to about 45-50 m.
- Fields in the Great Plains are bright green in November instead of the brown of harvested crops.
- The tuning menu (D-pad right / F4) was tested by its model test and by loading a saved setup in the Shipping build, not by hand by the release gate.

Release gate fixes:

- **A new pad layout** — the clutch is on A (hold), the parking brake on B (hold), the gears on the bumpers (RB up, LB down), the gearbox switch on the right-stick click, the engine restart on D-pad left and the HUD on X. The clutch was on LB, the same finger as the brake trigger, and hard steering pressed the left stick (the HUD) by accident. D-pad right opens the tuning menu.
- **Steady head motion and g-meter** (`FSimCruiseHeadModel`, `FSimCruiseGMeter`) — they divided the velocity change by the frame time while the 360 Hz physics gives 5, 6 or 7 new steps in a 60 fps frame, so they jittered by about 17 %. They now use the physics time (`SimCruise.Gameplay.Camera.PhysicsTimeNoJitter`).
- **No camera swoop after a recover** — the reset happens on the physics thread a frame or more after the request, so the snap came too early; the camera now snaps on the frame the car is seen to jump or turn upright.
- **Frame pacing** — a 60 fps cap (`simcruise.FrameCap`, 0 for none), and the RTX 2070 Super Max-Q and other Max-Q laptop GPUs start on the Medium preset: in the Shipping build at 1080p, Ocean Drive from the driver's seat ran 57 fps (1 % low 44) on High and 77 fps (1 % low 61) on Medium. NVIDIA Reflex low latency is available (`simcruise.Reflex 1`) but off by default: with it on, the Shipping game hung at exit; it is now turned off before the engine exits.
- **Crash bench** (`SimCruise.Vehicle.Crash.<car>`) — each release car hits a wall at 60 km/h, a tree at 50 km/h, a guard rail at 80 km/h and a stopped car at 50 km/h: no launch, no tunnelling, no NaN, and the reset drives on.
- **Release tools** — `-SimCruiseLatencyProbe` (stick to picture), `-SimCruiseSoak=<minutes>` (an unattended drive with crashes, recovers, camera, car and place changes), `scripts\release.ps1` (Shipping package, map helper, installer, checksums and smoke test in one step), `docs/release/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md` for every release, and the drive robot now drives the Mustang and the Corvette too.
- **The map helper is built with the package** (`scripts/package.ps1` builds `tools/atlas.exe` from `helper/atlas.mjs` when Bun is installed), so a package never ships an older helper.

Everything else in 2.0.0, since 2.0.0-preview.1:

- **True field of view from the driver's seat** (`SimCruiseLens::TrueHorizontalFovDeg`, road atlas Screen / Eye to screen) — a driver lens that is the angle the screen subtends at the eye, from the screen's diagonal and the eye's distance (15.6 in at 60 cm: 32.1° across), on F6, the left-stick click and the atlas. The default stays the natural lens (45° tall): on a laptop the true field of view reads as a head against the windscreen (the saved choice moved to `lens_v3`, so earlier saved lenses reset). `SimCruise.Gameplay.Lens.TrueFov` checks the geometry.
- **Night roads are lit** (`ASimCruiseVehicle::NightAdaptationLuminanceCdM2`, low-beam cone and range) — the eye adapted to a grey card facing the lamps (about 2 cd/m2), so the 0.1 cd/m2 road was nearly black; it now adapts to 0.5 cd/m2, inside real lamp-lit asphalt's 0.4-2. The low beam's cone no longer dims it a second time (core 30°, edge 32°: its shape comes from the cut-off pattern) and it reaches 250 m, not 122 m.
- **The engine's pitch runs to its own limiter** — the synth clamped every engine's pitch at 7200 rpm; it now follows to 200 rpm past the car's fuel cut. `SimCruise.Audio.Limiter` checks each car's stutter.
- **A manual clutch that locks** (`FSimCruiseVehicleSim::Step`) — with the pedal up the clutch was a viscous coupling that always slipped by torque over 10 N m s (about 2 % at half throttle in 3rd). It now locks as a dry clutch does: up to its capacity it carries the engine's torque, at the stiffest coupling a physics substep can take without overshooting (the automatic's 1/(dt I) cap), with this substep's engine torque included; part-way down it slips as before. 0.6 % in the same test (`SimCruise.Vehicle.Model.ManualClutchLocks`).
- **Engine braking for the NB** (`engine_friction_nm` 12, `engine_friction_per_rpm` 0.005) — the 1.8 L four's friction was 16 N m at 3000 rpm; a typical friction mean effective pressure of about 1.9 bar gives 27, so lifting off slows the car as the real one does. Full-throttle torque is unchanged.

- **No more hitch when a map tile loads** (`ASimCruiseWorldTile::ContinueInstall`, `ASimCruiseWorldStreamer`) — a 550 m tile arriving took 26-136 ms of one frame (collision sections 10-103 ms, ground mask and materials 15-32, its aerial photograph on top), a visible stutter at 40 fps. The photograph now gets a frame of its own, and the rest runs as steps (ground mask, one collision section, palms, signs) within about 6 ms a frame; a tile is not reported collision-ready until its steps are done. The hidden collision mesh no longer gets a scene proxy, so its GPU copy of the tile is gone too. Worst install frame now 35 ms, most steps 10-20 ms.
- **Banking runs on across tile seams** (`FSimCruiseRoadNetwork::Build`) — superelevation ran out to flat over the last 30 m of every tile, so long bends lost their banking at each seam. A seam end now eases to the neighbour's banking, and an end without a neighbour keeps its own. Tertiary (55 km/h design) and unclassified roads (45 km/h) now bank their bends as rural roads do (AASHTO 3.3); residential and service streets keep their crown.
- **No traffic car inside yours at a new start** (`World/SimCruiseTraffic`) — after a start, "Drive here" or a recover, a traffic car could stand in or against your car (seen at Waikiki, on Oatman Road and in Big Sur): traffic spawned around the start before your car was placed. When your car jumps further in a frame than any car drives (a floating-origin move does not count), every traffic car within 20 m of it goes. The traffic soak test now places the player on a traffic car and checks none is within 15 m a frame later.

- **Tune your car** (`Gameplay/SimCruiseTuningUI`, `Vehicle/SimCruiseTuning`; D-pad Right or F4 standing still) — a setup menu in workshop units: front and rear spring rates (lb/in), damping (% of stock), ride height (mm), front and rear anti-roll bars (% of stock), engine torque (%), final drive and steering ratio, each inside a safe range around the real car, with the body roll it gives in degrees per g (the NB rolls 5.45 deg/g as it left the factory). Up/Down choose, Left/Right change, X resets the row, Y pressed twice puts the whole car back as the real one, A saves it for that car (`Saved/SimCruise/tuning-<car>.json`) and applies it, B cancels. Ride height moves the suspension mounts in the body, so the body and its centre of mass really sit lower over the road (the lowered car is now lowered the same way: before, its body only looked lower). More than stock torque comes with a clutch that holds it. Checks, captures and the drive test always drive the real car (`-SimCruiseRealCar` too).
- **Steering compliance** (`steering_stiffness_nm_per_deg`, default 400) — the front tyres' aligning torque now winds the steering a little, so the road wheels turn slightly less than the rack asks, as in a real car. It is a real share of a road car's understeer: the NB goes from 0.65 to 1.31 deg/g, nearer the 2.6 deg/g European road-car average; every car's CarTrack figures stay in band.
- **The TMeasy drive force converges with the physics rate** — a driven tyre's force is taken at the substep's mean slip (two passes), so a launch gives the same 0-60 at 360 and 720 Hz.

- **Fix: random starts are not dropped for one slow tile** — the map helper failed a whole tile when Windows refused to delete a cache file another helper process had open, and one failed tile made the game give the place up after 30 s (seen on Woodward Avenue, Detroit). Cache trimming now skips a file it cannot remove, and a place is given up only after four tile failures or 90 s.
- **Each engine's own rev limiter** (`FSimCruiseInlineFourSynth::FInputs::LimiterRpm`) — the engine sound stuttered at 6950 rpm on every car; it now cuts where the car's powertrain does (`fuel_cut_rpm`: Miata 7250, Mustang 5000, Corvette 6600 rpm).
- **No more slow motion below 30 fps** (`MaxPhysicsDeltaTime=0.1` in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`) — the engine let physics catch up at most 1/30 s per frame, so whenever the game ran under 30 fps the whole world ran slower than real time: at 15 fps the car moved at half speed (2 s of physics in 4 s of real time), and it felt slow, floaty and late on a weak GPU. Physics may now catch up a frame of up to 0.1 s; at 12 fps the drive test measures 12.67 s of physics in 12.66 s of real time. The drive robot's `-LowFps` run now really caps the frame rate (`-SimCruiseDriveTestMaxFps=N`).

- **Promenades** (`SimCruiseTileDetail::BuildPromenades`) — Lummus Park's beach walk had no benches, lamps or seawall. From the map data anywhere: a low coral-stone seawall with a cap where a footway runs along sand (on the sand side), and along footways 3 m and wider a bench every 24 m on the land side and a white drum lamp post every 30 m (which joins the night light pool). Realism score at the Lummus Park photo: 47.4 (47.0 before; structure 52.9 to 55.1).
- **Corvette gearing and magazine-style timing** (`meridian_z.json`, `target_0_60_rollout`) — the C8 now has the real TREMEC TR-9080 ratios (2.91/1.76/1.22/0.88/0.65/0.51/0.40/0.33; ours were wrong from third up), and the bench can time 0-60 as Car and Driver does, after the first foot of rollout (0.31 s of the C8's launch). The C8 reads 3.22 s that way against their 2.8 s, an open item on launch grip. The Model 3 moves to the TMeasy tyre with its motor torque re-estimated (415 N m): 5.31 s, 164.9 ft, 0.884 g against 5.4 s, 165 ft, 0.88 g.
- **Cockpit settings on the road atlas** (`Gameplay/SimCruiseAtlasUI`) — as the Godot client had: buttons in the atlas's top bar set the sound, the HUD level, head motion (100, 50 or 0 %) and pad rumble strength (100, 60, 30 or 0 %), and the game remembers them.
- **Sidewalk cafes** (`SimCruiseTileDetail::BuildBuildings`, `detail:outdoor_seating`) — Ocean Drive's hotels and restaurants set tables under market umbrellas on their sidewalks, and the game had none. A building with a hotel, restaurant, cafe, bar or ice-cream place on it now sets a table under an octagonal umbrella (2.6 m across, one colour per building) every 3.2 m along its street front, on the porch when it has one and clear of the carriageway (three Art Deco buildings in four, one in three elsewhere).
- **The understeer gradient, measured** (`SimCruise.Vehicle.LiftOff.*`) — the lift-off bench's steady circles now give each car's understeer gradient by ISO 4138's constant-radius method (the extra steer per g between 0.3 and 0.5 g) and fail a car that oversteers or understeers past 8 deg/g. First numbers: NB 0.65 deg/g (lowered NB 0.41), CR-V 0.82, Odyssey 0.77, Grand Cherokee 0.47, Raptor 2.40, Mustang 3.14, Corvette 2.13, Model 3 0.12. Surveys put road cars at about 2.6 deg/g (European) to 4.4 (American), so the newer cars are close to neutral: the sim has no steering compliance yet (the aligning torque twisting the column takes away steer, typically worth about 1 deg/g), the next physics item.
- **The understeer gradient, measured** (`SimCruise.Vehicle.LiftOff.*`) — the lift-off bench's steady circles now give each car's understeer gradient by ISO 4138's constant-radius method (the extra steer per g between 0.3 and 0.5 g) and fail a car that oversteers or understeers past 8 deg/g. - **Snow on the near ground** (`World/SimCruiseRegionalTerrain`, ground shader) — the driving tiles round the car now take the same week's MODIS snow cover as the land to the horizon: settled snow over the ground, thinner where grass and shrubs poke through, with the road kept clear as a ploughed road is. Red Mountain Pass on US-550 has snow on both slopes in November.
- **Buildings with no height in the map get a believable one** (`SimCruiseTileDetail::BuildBuildings`) — the helper writes 5 m for a footprint with no measured or tagged height, which was 97 of the 151 buildings on the Ocean Drive start tile, so its three- and four-storey hotels stood as two-storey strips. Such a building now takes the lower-quartile height of the measured buildings within 120 m (the measured ones are the notable, taller ones), a storey more or less, up to 14 m; a hotel has at least three storeys and a house at most two. It works in any town with some measured buildings.
- **Blade signs and painted floor bands on Art Deco hotels** — a hotel without a central tower may carry its name letter over letter on a blade standing out of its front, edged in neon (the Breakwater, the Colony), and some hotels have painted bands in their trim colour along each floor line (`detail:vertical_sign`, `detail:parapet_bands`).
- **Trees in palm country** (`World/SimCruiseTrees`) — South Florida and Hawaii kept only the palms planted on mapped lawns, so Waikiki's streets had no trees. The measured crowns off the lawns are now half coconut palms (the tiles' palm variants, picked and scaled by the measured height, solid) and half broadleaf shade trees.
- **More cars on the TMeasy tyre** (`scripts/fit-tyres.ps1`) — the 1999 roadster, the 1965 Mustang (a bias-ply shape: 9.5 per radian, peak near 12 degrees), the CR-V, the F-150 Raptor, the Grand Cherokee SRT8 and the Odyssey now use the research tyre model, each with its grip fitted by the bench to its published 70-0 stop and skidpad (all within 1.5 % after two rounds; for example the Raptor 205.4 ft / 0.686 g against 206 ft / 0.68 g). The fit script runs the CarTrack bench and scales the four grip levels until the figures match. The Corvette and the Model 3 keep the old curve for now: on TMeasy the Corvette's launch converges poorly across physics rates and the Model 3 reaches 60 mph 0.2 s early, both to be looked at with their traction control.

- **Snow on the distant mountains** (`World/SimCruiseRegionalTerrain`) — the land to the horizon is shaded with summer photographs, so the Rockies had no snow in November. The helper's `snow` (NASA GIBS MODIS Terra NDSI snow cover, 500 m) gives the newest day within a week (GIBS publishes two to four days late) over the landscape's 90 km square, and the landscape whitens where snow lies, as the Godot client's regional snow did. The streamed driving tiles do not show snow yet.
- **A tyre is a tread, not a point** (`FSimCruiseVehicleSim::Step`, `contact_rays`) — each tyre found the ground with one ray at its centre, so a kerb under its edge did nothing until the centre reached it and a pothole narrower than the tread dropped the wheel. Now three rays (the centre and both shoulders, 40 % of the section width out) find it: a step of more than 1 cm under a shoulder carries the tyre, the contact stays under the wheel's centre so a rolling body does not widen the track, and the grip is the tread's mean (a tyre half on gravel grips in between). On even ground nothing changes: every car's track figures are as before.

- **Wall colours measured from each building's own photograph** (`Knowledge/SimCruisePlacePhotos`) — for buildings with a Wikidata photograph (P18), the wall colour is measured in the facade band of a recent colour photo on Wikimedia Commons (1990 or later; postcards left out, as their colours are not today's paint) and used over the palette, with the photo's author and licence kept as its source. 67 start-tile buildings have one (the Delano, the Ritz Plaza and the Breakwater white, the Tides pale yellow); a building with both a postcard and a recent photo uses the photo.
- **You start in your own Miata** (`nb2001_lowered`, `Config/DefaultGame.ini` `StartCarId`) — the drive now opens in "2001 Miata": the physics lane's lowered car on 205/50R15 tyres, with the owner's look moved onto it (top down, roll bar, low grey wing, darker green) and mesh wheels built from numbers (`wheel_design`: a dished cross-spoke face, a polished lip, a dark centre cap, sized from the car file). The stock 2001 roadster is back to its factory look.
- **A drive robot** (`scripts/drive-robot.ps1`) — the scripted gamepad driver as a nightly check: the limit manoeuvres on the flat pad for the stock NB and the lowered car, a history of peak rear-axle slip and yaw rate per manoeuvre per commit in `Saved/DriveTest/history.csv`, and a failure if the car spins where a real one stays in control. First run: both cars pass (part-trigger junction turns 3-8 degrees of rear slip, 80 km/h lane change under 3).
- **You start in your own Miata** (`nb2001_lowered`, `Config/DefaultGame.ini` `StartCarId`) — the drive now opens in "2001 Roadster (lowered)": the physics lane's lowered car on 205/50R15 tyres, with the owner's look moved onto it (top down, roll bar, low grey wing, darker green) and mesh wheels built from numbers (`wheel_design`: a dished cross-spoke face, a polished lip, a dark centre cap, sized from the car file). The stock 2001 roadster is back to its factory look.

- **Shrubs on the rangeland** (`World/SimCruiseTrees`) — the western deserts and prairies were bare ground. The helper's `shrub-cover` (USDA Rangeland Analysis Platform shrub cover and USGS RCMAP shrub height, 2025, west of about 87.5 W) gives each 34 m cell its shrub cover and height; the cell now holds that share of low, sage-green crowns of that height (up to sixteen drawn per cell, within 250 m), kept off the road, as the Godot client's rangeland did.
- **Live air and wind on the car** (`FSimCruiseVehicleSim::AirDensityKgPerM3`, `WindMps`, `USimCruiseVehicleMovementComponent::SetAir`) — drag used sea-level air at 15 °C and still air everywhere. The air density now follows the ISA pressure at the car's height above sea level and the live temperature (about 1.17 kg/m³ on a 30 °C Miami afternoon, 1.0 at 2,000 m in Colorado), and the drag acts on the car's speed through the air: the live wind at about 0.7 of its 10 m speed (car height) slows the car into a headwind, helps it with a tailwind and pushes it sideways in a crosswind.

- **Bridge decks** (`World/SimCruiseBridges`) — a bridge was a bare road ribbon over the gap. Each span now has a 1.2 m concrete deck with its fascia, a solid 0.85 m parapet on each side (the US 32-inch barrier) and a pier every 35 m where the deck stands more than 4 m above the ground, as the Godot client's bridge decks had. `-SimCruiseNoBridgeDecks` turns them off.
- **The tyres' aligning torque** (`SimCruiseDynamics::TmeasyPneumaticTrailM`, `FSimCruiseVehicleTelemetry::RackAligningTorqueNm`) — what a steering wheel feels was not computed. Each tyre's side force now acts a trail behind its steering axis: TMeasy's pneumatic trail (Chrono's lever 0.178 of the contact patch, the patch 2 sqrt(R d) from the tyre's deflection; about 22 mm on the NB) plus the caster's mechanical trail. On the NB the torque peaks near 3.5 degrees of slip, well before the side force (8.5 degrees), and falls as the front slides, as a real wheel goes light. Per wheel in the telemetry (`AligningTorqueNm`) and summed over the fronts for wheel force feedback; the drive test logs it (`rack_mz_nm`).

- **A local AI reads about the place, on the CPU** (`Knowledge/SimCruisePlaceAI`, [docs/place-graph.md](docs/place-graph.md#local-ai-for-the-gaps)) — where the open data says nothing, a small language model (Ollama on localhost, CPU only, so the GPU keeps rendering) reads the Wikipedia articles about places within 1.5 km (free API, CC BY-SA) and answers in the place graph's own words: the styles with shares, the main decade, the palette, the roof form. One answer per 2 km area, cached; the start areas ship theirs. Reading beats guessing: from memory a 4 B model called Ocean Drive Pueblo Revival of 1967; reading the extracts it says Art Deco 0.75 and MiMo 0.25, 1930s. The answer is weak evidence (below a building's own data and its dated neighbours). No local model: the graph works without it; `-SimCruiseNoPlaceAI` turns it off.
- **Town buildings anywhere in the US** (`SimCruiseTileDetail::BuildBuildings`, place graph recipes) — outside Miami Beach every building was the same flat box. The place graph's recipes now shape them: a house gets a hip or gable roof at a 6:12 pitch with 0.35 m eaves and soffits (asphalt shingle, or clay tile where `detail:tile_roof` says so) when its footprint nearly fills its box; a Main Street block is brick in three of five, with a projecting cornice under its coping, a belt course over its shopfronts and the tall storeys of a 19th-century commercial block (4.2 m, so a 12 m block shows three rows of windows, not four; the building shader now reads each building's storey height); a downtown tower of 20 m and more gets a glass curtain wall with mullions every 1.5 m and a spandrel at every floor over a lobby storey. Named shops sign their fronts there too (King Street, Charleston: Apple, Ladybird Books). New preview shots `street` and `streetside` work at any `-SimCruiseStart`.
- **The road surface and the rain reach the tyres** (`ASimCruiseWorldTile::SurfaceGripAtFace`, `USimCruiseVehicleMovementComponent::SetRoadWetness`) — every surface had dry-asphalt grip. Each tyre now reads the surface under it from the face its ground trace hits: asphalt and concrete 1.0, gravel (unpaved roads, rural shoulders) 0.7, the ground beside the road 0.8 of dry asphalt, and the live road wetness takes up to 30 % from asphalt, 19 % from the ground and 10 % from gravel (Wong, *Theory of Ground Vehicles*, table 1.3). The drive telemetry's `*_grip` columns show it.
- **The owner's own Miata** (`data/cars/nb2001_lowered.json`) — the owner's 2001 NB as it is: lowered about 35 mm on coilovers (7 / 5 kg/mm springs), 205/50R15 200-treadwear tyres on TMeasy with more grip and a stiffer curve. The stock `nb2001` keeps the published-test calibration. Bench: 0-60 7.7 s, 70-0 157 ft, skidpad 0.986 g, against estimated targets (0.98 g and 160 ft, typical of a lowered NB on 200TW tyres) until this car is measured.
- **Traffic stops at red lights** (`World/SimCruiseTraffic`) — the new traffic signals' stop line (`ASimCruiseSignals::IsStopFor`, red, or yellow too late to stop) is a stop line to the traffic, as a stop sign's is.
- **A place knowledge graph decides the details** (`Knowledge/`, `data/knowledge/place-graph.json`, [docs/place-graph.md](docs/place-graph.md)) — which building gets eyebrow ledges, fins, a neon name, porches, round corners, which colours and how much wear now follows from facts, not from a Miami box and fixed dice. Facts come from the tile (height, footprint, named places), the region (15 regions with climate; latitude bands elsewhere), open data per tile (OpenStreetMap tags through Overpass, ODbL; Wikidata architectural style, year, architect and heritage, CC0) and the live feeds. 75 rules in one data file join them (Art Deco hotel -> neon sign 0.9; Streamline Moderne -> round corners 0.95; desert -> earth-tone stucco), and a building with no data takes the style and era of its dated neighbours. The Carlyle now is Streamline Moderne of 1941 by Richard Kiehnel because Wikidata says so. A tile bakes in a few milliseconds on the build thread; the open data comes two tiles ahead of the car and is cached; the 25 start tiles ship theirs (`-run=SimCruisePlaceFacts`). The graph is also the hub of the live data: weather, air, sea, traffic counts and photographs become facts every 5 s (`light:neon_on` at night, `street:awnings_out` in the sun and in when the wind is strong). `SimCruise.Knowledge.Explain` tells why each building got each detail.
- **The co-driver names the buildings you pass** (`ASimCruiseHUD::DrawPlaceCard`) — driving past a building the fact store knows, a card above the minimap shows its name, style, year and architect, and its heritage listing, for 7 s.
- **The owner's Miata** (`CarPresentation.json` `nb2001`, profile `addons`) — the player roadster now looks like the owner's car in their photographs: very dark green metallic, top down (the soft top, its frame, rear window and door glass are taken off the model), a black roll bar with a diagonal brace and stays to the rear deck, and a low silver-grey wing on short uprights. Profiles can add simple parts the model lacks (tubes and boxes in the car's frame). Still to come: mesh wheels, Sparco seats, the lower stance.
- **USB steering wheels** (`Gameplay/SimCruiseWheelDevice`, the GameInput plugin for wheels only) — a racing wheel steers 1:1 at the car's real ratio, with its pedals, clutch, handbrake and paddles, and force feedback (speed-dependent centring and damping; `simcruise.Wheel.Force`). Pads stay on XInput. Untested on a physical wheel.
- **Gravel roads and traffic signals** (`FSimCruiseRoadNetwork`, `World/SimCruiseSignals`) — unpaved ways were left out of the road network, so rural gravel roads (Princeton Road in Nebraska, one of the presets) did not exist; they are back, drawn in gravel with no paint. Junctions where two through roads (trunk, primary, secondary or tertiary) cross now have traffic signals: a mast arm with a three-lamp LED head over each approach, cycling green 26 s, yellow 4 s and all-red 2 s per direction. Mapped signal positions need an Overpass server (`SIMCRUISE_OVERPASS_URL`); without one they follow this US practice. Traffic does not obey them yet (`ASimCruiseSignals::IsStopFor` is the hook). `-SimCruiseNoSignals` turns them off.
- **Hotels, bars and shops carry their real names** (`SimCruiseTileDetail::BuildBuildings`, Signs material) — buildings were blank boxes. Every named place in the map data now signs its own building on its street front: hotels and bars in neon, the others in painted letters on the fascia or on the awning's valance. - **Traffic no longer locks up at junctions** (`World/SimCruiseTraffic`, `SimCruise.World.TrafficSoak`) — with 40 cars on the Miami Beach streets for 8 minutes, the old rule left 29 cars standing for good: two or four cars at a junction each waited for the next (a ring), one waited for a car on a bridge above it, and queues grew behind them. Now only a car at an angle counts as crossing your path (not one in the next lane or coming the other way); two cars that block each other settle it as drivers do at an uncontrolled junction (the one with the other on its right gives way); in a ring the car that has stood longest goes first after 6 s; a car on another level is ignored; an all-way stop stops deferring after 10 s; and a car in a queue behind one that stands for good (you parked in the lane) turns off out of sight after 25 s.
